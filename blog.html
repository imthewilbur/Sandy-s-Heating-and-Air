5 Signs Your Furnace Needs Attention Before Winter Hits

Nobody wants a dead furnace in January. A few warning signs usually show up weeks before a full breakdown — catch them early and it's a quick fix instead of an emergency call.

Short cycling. If your furnace kicks on and off every few minutes instead of running a full cycle, something's telling it to stop early — often a dirty filter or a failing sensor.

Yellow pilot light. A healthy flame burns blue. Yellow can mean incomplete combustion, which is worth a same-day call, not a wait-and-see.

Rising energy bills with no other explanation. A furnace working harder than it should shows up on your bill before it shows up as a breakdown.

Strange smells or sounds. A little dust burning off at the season's first run is normal. Anything that lingers, or any banging, rattling, or screeching, isn't.

It's been more than a year since a tune-up. Most furnace failures we see start as a small, cheap problem that got ignored for a season or two.

If any of this sounds familiar, give us a call before the first hard freeze — we'd rather catch it now than fix it at 2am in a blizzard.

How Often Should You Really Change Your Air Filter?

This is the single most common question we get, and the honest answer is: it depends on your filter and your house, not a universal rule.

Standard 1-inch filters: every 1–3 months. If you've got pets, allergies, or run the system constantly, stick to the shorter end.

Thicker 4–5 inch media filters: every 6–12 months. These hold a lot more dust before they choke airflow.

The real test isn't the calendar — it's the filter itself. Pull it out and hold it up to a light. If you can't see light through it, it's overdue, whatever the calendar says.

A clogged filter isn't just a comfort issue. It forces your system to work harder, which means higher bills and more wear on parts that are expensive to replace. It's the cheapest maintenance you can do yourself — worth actually doing.

AC Not Cooling? Check These 3 Things Before You Call Us

Sometimes it's not a real problem — it's something you can check in five minutes. Here's what we'd ask you to look at before we send a truck out.

1. Check the thermostat. Make sure it's actually set to "cool," not just "fan," and that the temperature is set below the current room temp. Dead batteries in a thermostat cause more no-cool calls than you'd think.

2. Check the outdoor unit. Walk outside and look at the condenser. If it's covered in leaves, grass clippings, or grown over with weeds, airflow gets choked and cooling drops off fast. A quick clear-out sometimes solves it on the spot.

3. Check the air filter. Same as above — a filter so clogged that air can't move through it will make even a healthy AC struggle to cool the house.

If you've checked all three and it's still not cooling, that's when it's genuinely time to call. We'd rather you save the service fee on something simple — but if it's a real problem, we'll get someone out same-day.
